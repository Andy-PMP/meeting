# Labeling rubric

> You're answering one question: **if I were invited, what would I want done with this meeting?** Judge it as written. Don't guess what the organizer meant to write.

## 1. The three labels

### Keep the meeting (`keep`)
A topic that is ambiguous, nuanced, or emotionally charged, a situation so complex it requires live brainstorming, in-depth problem-solving or conflict resolution, or a decision that needs several people's input at once. Interviews, team/social events, and 1:1s about the person are always kept.

### Make it shorter (`shorter`)
A real decision or discussion, but more time or people than it needs, sometimes padded with unrelated status updates. If decisions are being made by a small subset of the attendees, the invitee list should be reduced.

### This is an email (`email`)
Simple status or progress updates, only one person speaking or presenting, no decisions needed, or no agenda and no stated purpose.

## 2. Signals

Check these in roughly this order. No single signal decides the label. Missing information isn't a signal: if an invite doesn't say how long it is or who's invited, skip that row rather than guessing.

| Signal | Points toward keep | Points toward shorter | Points toward email |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision needed? | Names a specific decision, and its owner or deadline ("decide by Friday") | A real decision, but a small part of a long agenda | No decision, or one person could decide alone |
| Real discussion vs. status updates | True back-and-forth discussion is needed ("back and forth", "bring both versions") | Real discussion is needed, but a small part of a long agenda | One person is presenting at the others ("I'll walk through", "I'll share"), or round-robin status updates |
| Attendees proportionate? *(skip if the invite doesn't show who's invited; people named with a job count as having a role)* | most attendees have a role | a real meeting, but a big chunk of the list has no role, or a broad group ("whole team", "all staff") when only a few people are needed ||
| Working session | People building, fixing, or generating ideas together |||
| Duration fits the agenda? *(skip if no duration is given)* | up to 15 minutes per obvious agenda item and decision (working sessions excepted) | over 15 minutes per obvious agenda item ||
| Agenda/purpose present? *A title that names a specific topic counts as a stated purpose; a generic title ('Sync', 'Catch up', 'Quick chat') doesn't.* | detailed, clear agenda is present | has vague agenda or stated purpose | no agenda and no stated purpose |
| Formal proceeding | The meeting itself is required by law, regulation, or policy (a compliance training, board vote, safety briefing), not just "attendance expected" | | |
| Hosted recording? | If time is given for live Q&A | | recorded, and no live Q&A |

## 3. Tie-breaks

Check these in this order, first matching rule wins.

- No agenda and no stated purpose → `email`, even if the meeting type is usually worthwhile.
- Social or team-building meetings → `keep`.
- 1:1s about the person → `keep`; two people meeting about a task are judged like any meeting
- Formal proceeding (required by law, regulation, or policy) → `keep`.
- A real decision with far too many people or minutes → `shorter`, not `keep`.
- Live Q&A but hosted recording → `keep`.
- Can't choose after 30 seconds → `shorter` and add a note.

## 4. Worked examples

3 short examples per label, at least one of them borderline, with one line of reasoning each.

**keep**
1. 4-person panel, 30 min, must review interview feedback and select 4 of 8 candidates to move to the next round by Friday → keep: a time-boxed decision that needs the disagreement aired live.
2. a department-wide live demo of new HR tools with a live Q&A at the end, which will be recorded and hosted → keep: live Q&A allows for organic discussion and problem solving you can't get from a recording.
3. a six-person planning session, 60 min, 3 agenda items and one related decision needed from all → keep: treating the decision needed as a fourth agenda item to allow time for debate

**shorter**
1. a 60 minute meeting with 10 people, 3 agenda items → shorter: real discussion needed, but meeting is too long for number of agenda items.
2. a 60 min meeting with 12 people to determine ownership of a new project, no details given → shorter: this will need back-and-forth discussion but needs to be shorter or have a more detailed agenda
3. decision needed on vendor shortlist, 20 people, 15 min, 2 agenda items → shorter: the length is fine but there are too many people invited to this call for the number of agenda items.

**email**
1. a single, clear and simple question → email: this can be addressed by one person or in one message
2. a training that will be recorded and hosted for viewing, with no live Q&A → email: with no Q&A or interaction, a recorded format is more fitting
3. a 45 min meeting for a single person to review a new document or process to the team → email: one presenter, can be shared and reviewed completely offline if no decision needed

## 5. One-line cheat-sheet (shown on every labeling screen)

- **1 keep:** a live decision or real back-and-forth, a 1:1 about the person, social, formal proceeding, working session, interview, or live Q&A.
- **2 shorter:** a real purpose, but too long, with too many people, or with too much filler
- **3 email:** one-way information or a question; nobody needs to be in the room

## 6. Discard (`d`) when

It isn't a meeting invite, it's broken or garbled, it's inappropriate, or it's so contradictory that labeling it would just add noise.
